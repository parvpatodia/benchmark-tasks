# Flare a copper tube in a precision flaring tool

The robot flares the end of a short 3/8 in soft copper tube with a precision flare tool, following the maker's steps
(RIDGID instruction sheet EC42865, from step 4's "tube end even with top" to step 7). The tube stands in the 3/8
opening of the tool's closed flaring bar, its end 4 mm proud; the yoke rests near the hinged end, 0, 4 or 8 mm along the bar by seed. One arm presses the
tube's end even with the bar's top with closed fingers, slides the yoke over the tube until the arrow is on the 3/8
line and tightens the clamp screw with three strokes of its T-bar. The other arm then turns the feed handle clockwise in
half-turn strokes until the cone's clutch releases (the feed screw's red top, standing up through the handle's hub,
stops sinking), then two more full turns, then back until the cone's tip is 2 mm over the bar's top. Both arms let go.

What it tests is following a hand tool's procedure in order and to its tolerances. The end must be within 0.5 mm of
the bar's top when the flare forms. The arrow must be within 1 mm of the tube's axis. The clamp must be tight when the
cone meets the tube, or the flare is scrap. The handle must keep turning past the clutch's
release, and the cone must come back off. A flare is formed once, so a wrong order or a wrong setting cannot be undone.

Files: `flare_tool.py` (the task), `mechanism.py` (the scene builder), `task.json` (generated by
`tools/gen_taskjson.py`), `test_flare_tool.py`, and probes in `tools/`.

## Real trials

Same layout with a RIDGID 458R (or any 45 deg precision flare tool with a clutch), 3/8 in OD soft copper (Type L),
75 mm offcuts. Before the trials, flare 5 offcuts by hand to RIDGID's steps with the end
exactly even with the bar's top, measure each flare's outside diameter with calipers, and set the OD window to their
mean +-0.3 mm. After each trial, measure:

- the flare's OD with calipers at two diameters 90 deg apart: both in the window, and differing by 0.3 mm or less
  (concentric), is a good flare; anything else formed is scrap;
- ironed: the flare face smooth (no visible facets or tool marks) and the tool's clutch having slipped (a camera on the
  feed screw: the screw stops sinking while the handle turns, at least two turns more);
- retracted: the cone off the flare (a feeler under the cone); set and on the line read off the bar's lines with a
  ruler (1 mm); clamped: a paint mark on the T-bar's head, turned 0.4 turn or more past the point where the screw's
  tip first touches the bar (found once by hand with the tube in, before the trials);
- clear: both grippers 10 mm or more from the tube and the yoke, measured with a ruler after the run.

Score with the same ladder. A trial takes about 3 minutes with reset (put a fresh offcut in the 3/8 opening 4 mm proud and
close the bar on it, open the clamp and feed screws, park the yoke at the trial's place, T-bar up).

## Run

    PYTHONPATH=<yam-isaac-lab-arena checkout> python -m pytest -q -p no:cacheprovider test_flare_tool.py
    inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena --policy yam_scripted -E task=flare_tool.py -E object_jitter=0 \
        -E control_hz=20 --scorer task_score --max-steps 3000

`--seed` sets the layout (inspect-robots hashes it into the trial seed: `--seed 0` gives the cone 5 mm up and the
yoke 4 mm along, 2 gives 2 mm and 0 mm, 4 gives 8 mm and 8 mm). inspect-robots resets the policy before the scene,
so `flare_tool.py` has `yam_scripted` plan its demo again on each trial's first step, once the seed is known: the
scripted demo reads the seed (privileged), it does not sense the yoke. This touches only `yam_scripted`, not the
scene, other policies or the score. `sim/` holds the scripts used for the runs reported in `task.json` (`mk.sh` makes start-delay copies, `run.sh`,
`film.sh`, `dream.sh`).

## Sim results

yam_arena, scripted demo, `object_jitter=0`, 20 Hz, 3,000-step limit. The filmed demo (`--seed 0`: cone 5 mm up, yoke 4 mm along) scored 1.0 with success at step 2,314 (115.7 s) and took 1,133 s of wall time on a shared GPU. Traced runs of the same demo scored 1.0 on `--seed 2` (2 mm, 0 mm) and `--seed 4` (8 mm, 8 mm). The one Dream-1 run (`--seed 0`) scored 0 over 3,000 steps. After the runs only `task.json`'s `context` (the runs do not read it) and `scores` lines changed.

## Sim against real

- PhysX cannot form copper, so the forming is a model. The cone does not collide. A model run after every control
  step reads the handle's net angle (turning back undoes turning forward), sets the cone's height (2 mm per turn),
  stops it at the clutch depth, and counts turns past that as ironing. There is no torque on the handle or the
  T-bar; the clamp tip is posed from the T-bar's net turns and acts on the bar as a stiff spring. While the cone presses the
  tube's end before the flare forms, the model pushes a loose tube down with 15 N, so it slides and the flare is scrap.
  A tightened clamp holds the tube against the cone in the real tool; in the sim a 14 g tube squeezed between the stiff
  halves sinks into them instead, so once the clamp is 0.4 turn past contact the model stops pushing the tube. The flare is
  also scrap if the clamp was not tight when the cone first met the tube, or if the cone came down beside the tube and
  was then slid over it (its tip already 0.5 mm under the end). It records where the end stood when the
  cone reached full depth and whether the cone's axis was within 1 mm of the tube's at first contact. The flare is
  not drawn.
- The real bar has no closing spring. Here a light spring presses the hinged half on the tube so the bar holds it until
  it is clamped: its half-hole sits 0.1 mm into the tube's place, so the tube holds the half 1.1 mrad open and the
  spring grips it. That grip does not hold the tube against the cone's push. The tightened clamp does.
- The task starts with the tube already in the closed bar (RIDGID step 2, step 3 and the first half of step 4: loosening
  the screws, parking the yoke, opening the bar, putting the tube in and closing it are not part of it). Step 8, loosening the clamp and
  parking the yoke again, is not either: the flare is scored with the tool still clamped.
- No flare nut on the tube. In the field the nut goes on first; that step is not part of this task.
- The clamp screw is right-hand: tightening is clockwise seen from in front.
- RIDGID parks the yoke at the hinged end (here its clamp screw behind the hinge pin). The task starts it 0, 4 or 8 mm
  further along by seed, so a script that grips and slides it one fixed way ends off the 3/8 line.

## Tests

`test_flare_tool.py` checks the geometry with convex-hull gaps: nothing starts inside anything (the closed bar touches
the tube at rest), no collision hull is under 1 mm thick (PhysX may inflate a thinner one), the hinge's stop keeps the
bare faces apart, the closed bar grips the tube, the yoke slides over the set tube, and the clamp tip meets the bar
after a third of a turn, so the three T-bar strokes (1.36 turns) tighten it. It checks the forming model: the clutch,
ironing (counted only while the clamp is tight), retracting, the 0.5 mm end window, the 1 mm centring window, and the unclamped tube slipping. It checks the
scoring ladder and its latches (1.0 only after 1 s held; the yoke on the 3/8 line before any credit; a stalled model
scores 0), that a fixed 4 or 5.5 handle turns fails some seed, that a yoke slide scripted for one seed ends off
the line on the others, that a tube not clamped when the cone meets it or a cone slid on from the side scraps, (the demo turns 7.5, past every seed's clutch and two
more), that `task.json` is regenerated from the code, and the demo's IK (worst 0.1 mm over 116 s, both arms).
`tools/clearance.py` checks the demo's hand paths against every part with the tool posed along the plan, and
`tools/arms.py` every link of one arm against every link of the other (both offline, no sim).
