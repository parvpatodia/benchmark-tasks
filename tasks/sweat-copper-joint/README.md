# Sweat a copper water joint

The robot solders a vertical 1/2 in copper water-tube joint the way the Copper Development Association's guide
"Soldering and Brazing Copper Tube and Fittings" says. The joint is already cleaned, fluxed and assembled: a top tube
pushed into a wrought coupling on a tube held in a pipe vise (the coupling's lower joint is already soldered). One arm
takes a lit hose torch from its rest and holds the flame's inner cone on the base of the coupling's cup. The other arm
takes a small coil of 1/8 in 95-5 solder from its holder and touches the coil's straight end to the joint where the
top tube enters the coupling, opposite the torch (CDA: the molten solder is drawn "into the cup toward the heat
source"). While the joint is below the solder's liquidus (464 F) nothing melts. Once it is hot enough, the wire melts
in and the arm feeds about 8 mm of it, until a ring of solder shows round the joint's edge. Then both arms take their
tools away, keeping them in hand, and the joint cools until the solder sets.

The skill being tested is heat control judged by touch. The robot cannot see the joint's temperature, and how fast the
joint heats varies by seed: with the flame on its mark it heads toward 1,050 F, 850 F or 720 F. The coolest seed
reaches the liquidus after 23.3 s of steady heat; the hottest passes 600 F after 19.5 s. So no fixed timing works for
every seed: the wire melts in only once the joint is hot, and the robot has to notice that, feed, and take the heat
away before the flux burns. It must also lift the wire off before the joint cools through the solidus (452 F), or the
solder sets round it. A burnt joint cannot be saved. The flame must also stay off the joint's edge and off the solder
(CDA: "Do not overheat the joint or direct the flame into the face of the fitting cup").

Files: `solder_joint.py` (the task), `mechanism.py` (the scene builder), `task.json` (generated by
`tools/gen_taskjson.py`), `test_solder_joint.py`, offline probes in `tools/`, and sim scripts in `sim/`.

## Seeds

`inspect-robots --seed` sets the trial seed (`zlib.crc32(f"{cli}:None:0")` for the first trial). The heat rate is
`T_SS[(seed // 3) % 3]`: `--seed 0` gives 850 F, `--seed 2` 1,050 F, `--seed 4` 720 F. With the flame on its mark the
joint reaches 464 F after 13 s, 18 s or 23 s (1,050, 850, 720 F). The scripted demo reads the seed (it is privileged)
and times its feed from its own plan. inspect-robots resets the policy before the scene, so the task file makes the
scripted policy plan again on each trial's first action, after the reset has set the seed. This changes only the
scripted demo, never the scene or the score.

## Real trials

Hardware:

- Joint: 1/2 in Type L hard copper tube (ASTM B88), a 75 mm bottom piece and a 45 mm top piece, in a wrought copper
  coupling (ASME B16.22). Solder the bottom piece into the coupling's lower cup beforehand. Measure each top tube's OD
  and cup's ID and use only joints at 0.004 ± 0.001 in clearance (Table 1's column). Clean the top end and cup with
  abrasive cloth and a fitting brush, brush on Superior No. 142-5 flux, assemble, and clamp the bottom piece upright
  in a pipe vise so the coupling's mouth is 65 mm over the table.
- Solder: Harris 95-5, 1/8 in wire wound into a small coil (about 45 g) with a 55 mm straight end, marked every 1 mm
  from its tip. The end stands down in a 10 mm well in a block.
- Torch: a straight-tip propane hose torch, its flame along the handle's axis, fed through a 3 m hose from a regulator
  on a cylinder. Indoor use of a propane cylinder needs the lab's fire-safety approval (NFPA 58 and local code govern
  it; we have not read them), and the trials are not run without it. The hose runs taut down through the torch's
  rest (Protection). The valve stays on one mark for every trial, so the flame looks the same. The torch rests on a
  steel block. The operator lights it there just before the run and shuts it off at the end.
- Heat rate (the seed): a hidden heat sink, not the flame. A copper block (none, small or large) is clamped to the
  bottom tube under the vise jaws, behind a sheet-steel skirt, so no camera can see which one is fitted.
- Calibrate before the trials on sacrificial joints in the same vise, with the fume hood running (its draft changes
  the heat rate). A type K thermocouple is cemented to the upper cup's wall 90 deg round from the flame (not where the
  solder goes in), 3 mm under the mouth, and logged at 10 Hz. For each block, time the joint from 70 F to 464 F with
  the inner cone on the mark, and size the blocks so the times are 13 s, 18 s and 23 s (the sim's seeds). Also check:
  - with no block, the time to 600 F is at least 2 s before the large block's time to 464 F;
  - for each block, the time from 464 F to 600 F with the flame held on (the window the robot works in);
  - for each block, the cooling from the peak down to 200 F after the flame comes off, to fit the time constant (the
    sim keeps one time constant for all seeds);
  - that a wire held in the flame's wash 10 mm over a cold joint does not melt.
  - that the torch lit on its rest for 30 s (lighting to the first action) warms the cup by under 10 F [CHOICE].
- Protection: below.

How each reading is made:

- Temperature: the cup thermocouple logs the whole trial. A 600 F temperature-indicating crayon stripe on the upper
  cup 90 deg round from the flame, on the other side from the thermocouple, is a second check.
- Burnt flux (score 0): the log over 600 F at any time, or the crayon stripe melted.
- Flame on the joint's edge or the solder's end (score 0): from the overhead and side cameras against a scale, the
  inner cone's tip within 10 mm of the coupling's mouth or of the wire's tip. This is our stricter form of the CDA
  caution; it also bars the sweep between cup and tube that the CDA allows.
- Fed: the wire's length before less its length after, read from the 1 mm marks (to 0.5 mm). Drip check: the assembly
  is weighed on a 0.01 g scale before fluxing and again after the trial with the flux residue wiped off with a damp
  cloth. The gain, less the residue left on a fluxed, heated and wiped calibration joint with no solder, divided by
  the mass of 1 mm of the same wire (weigh a 100 mm piece), must be at least 80% of the length fed, or the solder
  dripped off and the joint scores as half full.
- Full: 8.1 mm or more fed, and a solder fillet all round the joint's edge, checked with an inspection mirror. A joint
  with 8.1 mm fed but a gap in the fillet scores as half full.
- Solder set round the wire (score 0): the wire is fused to the joint after the trial.
- Away and set: the flame's inner cone 8 cm or more from the mark (the cup's base on the torch side) and the wire's
  tip 3 cm or more from the centre of the coupling's mouth, read from the overhead camera against a scale, while the
  log falls under 452 F and stays there for 1 s. Each tool must be in hand or on its stand; a tool let go on the bench
  is not away. "On its stand" means within 2 cm of where it started; "held up" means 8 cm or more over the table.

Score with the same ladder: 1.0 success; 0.8 full, set and away but not held 1 s; 0.6 full; 0.5 half full (4.1 mm);
0.25 any solder melted in; 0 after a latched failure. To reset (about 10 minutes):

- shut off the torch;
- let the joint cool under 120 F, then cut it out and fit a fresh cleaned and fluxed assembly;
- let the vise cool to room temperature and fit the trial's heat-sink block;
- stand a new marked coil in the holder;
- light the torch on its rest.

the trip was the equipment's and the trial is voided instead. A voided trial is rerun on the same seed; a trial voided
twice stops the session until the cause is found. At the trial's 150 s limit (3,000 steps at 20 Hz, from the policy's
first action) the relay also closes the valve. That is the normal end, not a trip: the trial scores by the ladder at
that moment, as in the sim.

## Sim results

yam_arena, scripted demo, `object_jitter=0`, 20 Hz, 3,000-step limit. The filmed demo (`--seed 0`: heat rate toward
850 F) scored 1.0 with success at step 678 (33.9 s) and took 320 s of wall time on a shared GPU. Traced copies of the
same demo on all three seeds scored 1.0: `--seed 2` (1,050 F) at step 619, `--seed 0` (850 F) at step 675 and `--seed
4` (720 F) at step 768, with peaks of 540, 509 and 489 F. While pressing, the wire's centre stayed 9.75 to 10.50 mm
from the joint's axis (the edge zone ends at 10.69 mm), and the flame's tip came no nearer than 14.8 mm to the joint's
edge (the latch is at 10 mm). These traced runs used the code before two final scoring edits (the edge zone widened to
the coupling's 1.2 mm lip, and the tools-away rule made stricter); the demo's motion is the same, and both edits pass
on the traced end states. The one Dream-1 run (`--seed 0`) scored 0 over 3,000 steps. The film and the Dream-1 run
hashed `solder_joint.py`, `mechanism.py` and `task.json` before starting; the code hashes match the files here. After
the runs only `task.json`'s `context` (the runs do not read it) and `scores` lines changed.

## Sim against real

- PhysX has no heat, flame or melting. The joint's temperature is a model stepped after every control step:
  dT/dt = P_seed exp(-d^2 / (2 sigma^2)) - (T - 70 F) / tau. Here d is the distance from the torch's inner-cone tip to
  the flame's mark (the cup's base, facing the torch), sigma = 25 mm and tau = 25 s. The flame is drawn only.
- The wire does not shorten. Its end rests on the joint's face, a spring (0.8 N/mm) hidden just under the coupling's
  top.
  - Solder melts in only while all of these hold: the wire's inner edge is on the coupling's 1.2 mm lip (within 1.2 mm
    of the top tube); the lowest point of its end is within 0.5 mm of the face; it pushes the face 0.5 mm or more
    under the solder's level; and the joint is at or over 464 F.
  - The model then lowers the face at up to 5 mm/s (fed length), so the tip sinks into the joint as the arm pushes on.
    Melting needs real contact and pressure at the joint's edge, not proximity.
- The coupling's top cup is drawn only (its lower half collides), so the wire can sink. A silver ring rises at the
  joint's edge when it is full (the CDA's "drops"), and a dark ring if the flux burns.
- The flame is held still on one mark and the solder fed at one point. The CDA describes sweeping the flame round the
  joint and, for vertical joints, "a similar sequence of overlapping passes".
- Not modelled: capillary flow, the fillet's shape (sim "full" is fed length only), flux chemistry, the torch's hose,
  and the heat that travels up the wire.
- The real seed is a hidden heat sink and is checked by the thermocouple. The sim's heat rate is fixed by seed, with
  one cooling time constant for all seeds.
- Melting needs the wire's end on the face, but the sim does not check which body pushes the face down. A finger or
  the nozzle pressing it while the wire's end hovers within 0.5 mm would also feed. The demo's pads stay 58 mm above
  the wire's tip and its nozzle 25 mm behind the inner cone.
- The real trial has safety trips the sim does not (the Protection table). A policy that scores 1.0 in the sim can
  score 0 in a real trial if its path trips one: the flame near an arm, a wrist camera, the hose or a rig part, any
  checked point moving over 0.25 m/s, the flame's segment leaving the working box, the torch held off its grip
  section (the sim's handle has no sleeve), turned or slid in the grip, or tilted past 45 degrees, a hand or the
  distance sensor over its temperature trip, or the torch taken from its rest other than straight up by the left hand
  (the sim's rest has no rails). Offline, the scripted demo stays clear of the flame-axis (on the rest and off it),
  speed, box, rail and hose checks; the others are checked in the dry run and calibration.
- The real torch differs at its rear: it ends 58 mm behind the grip point, where the sim's handle runs on to 90 mm
  (on the rest its last 5.2 mm lie inside the left base's collision hull), and it carries a sensor case on top, a
  hose fitting underneath and about 2 N of pull from the hose's counterweight, which the sim does not have. The
  sim's rest block also overlaps the left base's mounting plate; the real one is cut away there (Protection).
