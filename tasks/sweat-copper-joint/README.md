# Sweat a copper water joint

The robot solders a vertical 1/2 in copper water-tube joint the way the Copper Development Association's guide
"Soldering and Brazing Copper Tube and Fittings" says. The joint is already cleaned, fluxed and assembled: a top tube
pushed into a wrought coupling on a tube held in a pipe vise (the coupling's lower joint is already soldered). One arm
takes a lit hose torch from its rest and holds the flame's inner cone on the base of the coupling's cup. The other arm
takes a small coil of 1/8 in 95-5 solder from its holder and touches the coil's straight end to the joint where the top
tube enters the coupling, opposite the torch (CDA: the molten solder is drawn "into the cup toward the heat source").
While the joint is below the solder's liquidus (464 F) nothing melts. Once it is hot enough, the wire melts in and the
arm feeds about 8 mm of it, until a ring of solder shows round the joint's edge. Then both arms take their tools away,
keeping them in hand, and the joint cools until the solder sets.

The skill being tested is heat control judged by touch. The robot cannot see the joint's temperature, and how fast the
joint heats varies by seed: with the flame on its mark it heads toward 1,050 F, 850 F or 720 F. The coolest seed reaches
the liquidus after 23.3 s of steady heat; the hottest passes 600 F after 19.5 s. So no fixed timing works for every
seed: the wire melts in only once the joint is hot, and the robot has to notice that, feed, and take the heat away
before the flux burns. It must also lift the wire off before the joint cools through the solidus (452 F), or the solder
sets round it. A burnt joint cannot be saved. The flame must also stay off the joint's edge and off the solder (CDA:
"Do not overheat the joint or direct the flame into the face of the fitting cup").

Files: `solder_joint.py` (the task), `mechanism.py` (the scene builder), `task.json` (generated by
`tools/gen_taskjson.py`), `test_solder_joint.py`, offline probes in `tools/`, and sim scripts in `sim/`.

Our rules and estimates (in `solder_joint.py` and `task.json` `setup`):

- The flux "burns" over 600 F. This is our rule, set at the top of the flux's rated range. The sheet does not say it
  burns there.
- Estimates: the coupling's 1.2 mm wall, the torch's mass and shape, the flame's 25 mm inner cone, the heat model's
  time constant and falloff, the three heat rates, and the 5 mm/s feed limit.

## Seeds

`inspect-robots --seed` sets the trial seed (`zlib.crc32(f"{cli}:None:0")` for the first trial). The heat rate is
`T_SS[(seed // 3) % 3]`: `--seed 0` gives 850 F, `--seed 2` 1,050 F, `--seed 4` 720 F. With the flame on its mark the
joint reaches 464 F after 13 s, 18 s or 23 s (1,050, 850, 720 F). The scripted demo reads the seed (it is privileged)
and times its feed from its own plan. inspect-robots resets the policy before the scene, so the task file makes the
scripted policy plan again on each trial's first action, after the reset has set the seed. This changes only the
scripted demo, never the scene or the score.

How each reading is made:

- Temperature: the cup thermocouple logs the whole trial. A 600 F temperature-indicating crayon stripe on the upper cup
  90 deg round from the flame, on the other side from the thermocouple, is a second check.
- Burnt flux (score 0): the log over 600 F at any time, or the crayon stripe melted.
- Flame on the joint's edge or the solder's end (score 0): from the overhead and side cameras against a scale, the
  inner cone's tip within 10 mm of the coupling's mouth or of the wire's tip. This is our stricter form of the CDA
  caution; it also bars the sweep between cup and tube that the CDA allows.
- Fed: the wire's length before less its length after, read from the 1 mm marks (to 0.5 mm). Drip check: the
  assembly is weighed on a 0.01 g scale before fluxing and again after the trial with the flux residue wiped off with a
  damp cloth. The gain, less the residue left on a fluxed, heated and wiped calibration joint with no solder, divided
  by the mass of 1 mm of the same wire (weigh a 100 mm piece), must be at least 80% of the length fed, or the solder
  dripped off and the joint scores as half full.
- Full: 8.1 mm or more fed, and a solder fillet all round the joint's edge, checked with an inspection mirror. A joint
  with 8.1 mm fed but a gap in the fillet scores as half full.
- Solder set round the wire (score 0): the wire is fused to the joint after the trial.
- Away and set: the flame's inner cone 8 cm or more from the mark (the cup's base on the torch side) and the wire's tip
  3 cm or more from the centre of the coupling's mouth, read from the overhead camera against a scale, while the log
  falls under 452 F and stays there for 1 s. Each tool must be in hand or on its stand; a tool let go on the bench is
  not away. "On its stand" means within 2 cm of where it started; "held up" means 8 cm or more over the table.

Score with the same ladder: 1.0 success; 0.8 full, set and away but not held 1 s; 0.6 full; 0.5 half full (4.1 mm);
0.25 any solder melted in; 0 after a latched failure. To reset (about 10 minutes):

- shut off the torch;
- let the joint cool under 120 F, then cut it out and fit a fresh cleaned and fluxed assembly;
- let the vise cool to room temperature and fit the trial's heat-sink block;
- stand a new marked coil in the holder;
- light the torch on its rest.

## Sim against real

- PhysX has no heat, flame or melting. The joint's temperature is a model stepped after every control step:
  dT/dt = P_seed exp(-d^2 / (2 sigma^2)) - (T - 70 F) / tau. Here d is the distance from the torch's inner-cone tip to
  the flame's mark (the cup's base, facing the torch), sigma = 25 mm and tau = 25 s. The flame is drawn only.
- The wire does not shorten. Its end rests on the joint's face, a spring (0.8 N/mm) hidden just under the coupling's
  top.
  - Solder melts in only while all of these hold: the wire's inner edge is on the coupling's 1.2 mm lip (within 1.2 mm of the top tube); the lowest
    point of its end is within 0.5 mm of the face; it pushes the face 0.5 mm or more under the solder's level; and the
    joint is at or over 464 F.
  - The model then lowers the face at up to 5 mm/s (fed length), so the tip sinks into the joint as the arm pushes on.
    Melting needs real contact and pressure at the joint's edge, not proximity.
- The coupling's top cup is drawn only (its lower half collides), so the wire can sink. A silver ring rises at the
  joint's edge when it is full (the CDA's "drops"), and a dark ring if the flux burns.
- The flame is held still on one mark and the solder fed at one point. The CDA describes sweeping the flame round the
  joint and, for vertical joints, "a similar sequence of overlapping passes".
- Not modelled: capillary flow, the fillet's shape (sim "full" is fed length only), flux chemistry, the torch's hose,
  and the heat that travels up the wire.
- The real seed is a hidden heat sink and is checked by the thermocouple. The sim's heat rate is fixed by seed, with
  one cooling time constant for all seeds.
- Melting needs the wire's end on the face, but the sim does not check which body pushes the face down. A finger or
  the nozzle pressing it while the wire's end hovers within 0.5 mm would also feed. The demo's pads stay 58 mm above
  the wire's tip and its nozzle 25 mm behind the inner cone.
